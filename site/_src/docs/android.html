Android setup

The Android app keeps a history of what you copy on your phone and shares it with your computer. Android limits clipboard access more than desktops do, so here is how saving works and the few taps it needs.

{{i:info}}

Not on the Play Store. Download the APK from the download page and install it yourself. Tested on a Samsung Galaxy S23 FE (One UI 8, Android 16); other phones should work, though menu names may differ.

How saving works

Since Android 10, only the keyboard or the app on screen may read the clipboard. {{B}} doesn't ask you to switch keyboards or turn on an accessibility service, so it saves a copy when you ask it to, with one tap:

  • the Save clipboard Quick Settings tile;
  • long-press the {{B}} icon → Save clipboard (drag it out for a one-tap home screen button), or the Save clipboard widget;
  • Share → Save to {{B}} from any app (text and images), or select text and pick Save to {{B}} from the selection menu;
  • opening {{B}}, or its save button;
  • optionally, a Save clipboard button on a {{B}} notification.

Going the other way needs nothing: once the two are paired, what you copy on your computer shows up in the phone's history by itself. Tap it to copy.

On Android 12 and newer you'll see a short "{{B}} pasted from your clipboard" message after each save. That's Android telling you an app read the clipboard; it's expected.

1. Install the APK

  1. Download the .apk from the Download page on your phone.
  2. Open it. If asked, allow Install unknown apps for your browser or file manager, then tap Install.
  3. Requires Android 10 or newer.

2. Add a one-tap Save button

Pick whichever suits you; they all do the same thing.

  • Quick Settings tile: swipe down twice from the top of the screen, tap the ✎ edit (pencil) button, and drag Save clipboard into your active tiles.
  • App icon shortcut: long-press the {{B}} icon and tap Save clipboard. Drag that entry onto the home screen to keep it there as its own button.
  • Widget: long-press an empty part of the home screen → Widgets → {{B}} → Save clipboard.

Optional: Save button in notifications

Turn on Settings → Capture → Save button in notifications to keep a quiet "{{B}} is ready" notification with a Save clipboard button. It also keeps sync with your computer running while the app is closed. It's off by default.

3. Keep it running (Samsung)

Only needed if you turned on the notification above, or want sync to keep running in the background.

Samsung (and Xiaomi, OnePlus, Huawei) phones aggressively stop background apps. On a Samsung Galaxy:

  1. When the app asks "Let app always run in background?", tap Allow. Or: Settings → Apps → {{B}} → Battery → Unrestricted.
  2. Settings → Battery → Background usage limits → Never sleeping apps → + → add {{B}}.
  3. On the same screen, turn off Put unused apps to sleep.

Other brands: look for "Autostart", "No restrictions" or "Don't optimise" in the app's battery settings. dontkillmyapp.com has per-brand instructions.

4. Pair with your computer

  1. Make sure the phone is on the same Wi-Fi as your computer.
  2. On the computer: Settings → Devices → Pair a phone.
  3. On the phone: open {{B}}, choose pairing and scan the QR code.
  4. Check that the 4-digit check code matches on both screens.

From then on, copies on either device appear in the other's history. On the phone, tap a clip to copy it, then paste anywhere. Details in Sync your devices.

Privacy on Android

  • {{B}} reads the clipboard only when you ask it to (the button, tile, share or text menu, or opening the app). It has no accessibility service and never sees your screen.
  • Clips marked sensitive by the app you copied from (Android's "sensitive" flag, which password managers set) are skipped.
  • Sync keys stay on the phone, in the app's private storage. The app only talks to your own hub on your local network.
