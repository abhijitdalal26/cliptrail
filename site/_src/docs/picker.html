The quick picker & paste-back

The picker is {{B}}'s fastest path: a hotkey, a few letters, Enter. The clip lands in the app you were already typing in.

Opening the picker

  • Press Ctrl Shift Space anywhere. It opens centred on your screen. You can change the hotkey in Settings → Shortcuts.
  • Or left-click the tray icon: the picker opens as a flyout next to it.
  • Or choose Quick picker from the tray menu (right-click).

Press Esc, or click anywhere else, to close it without pasting.

Finding a clip

  • Type to search. Matching is by substring, anywhere in the text, file paths or source app name, and it isn't fooled by word boundaries: ploy finds deploy.sh.
  • Switch kind with Tab and Shift Tab: All, Pinned, Links, Code, Text, Images, Files, Colors, Contacts, Sensitive.
  • Move with ↑ ↓, or Page Up Page Down for bigger jumps.
  • Pin the selected clip with Ctrl P so it's never cleaned up and always under Pinned.

Pasting

KeysWhat happens
EnterPastes the selected clip into the app you were in, keeping formatting (rich text, images, files).
Shift EnterPastes as plain text, dropping any formatting.
Ctrl 1 … Ctrl 9Pastes one of the first nine items directly; the hint is shown on each row.
Ctrl Enter or →Opens the action list for the clip (clean link, copy as RGB, prettify JSON…). Pick one with ↑ ↓ and Enter to paste the result. ← or Esc goes back.
Ctrl DOpens the dashboard instead.

How paste-back works

When the picker opens, {{B}} remembers which window had focus. When you choose a clip, it:

  1. puts the clip on the system clipboard,
  2. hides the picker and gives focus back to that window,
  3. sends a Ctrl V keystroke.

The clip stays on your clipboard afterwards, so if paste-back can't reach a window you can always press Ctrl V yourself.

Where paste-back can't reach

  • Windows running as administrator (an elevated terminal, some installers): Windows doesn't let a normal app send keystrokes to them. Paste manually with Ctrl V, or run {{B}} elevated too (not recommended).
  • Linux on Wayland: Wayland doesn't allow apps to type into other windows, so paste-back is X11-only. On Wayland the picker copies the clip and tells you to press Ctrl V.
  • Terminals that paste with Ctrl Shift V (most Linux terminals): the synthetic Ctrl V does nothing there. Press Ctrl Shift V yourself; the clip is already on the clipboard. Windows Terminal accepts Ctrl V.
  • macOS: paste-back isn't implemented yet.
